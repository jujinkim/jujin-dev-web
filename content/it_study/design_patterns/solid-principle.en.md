In object-oriented development, there is an important set of principles called `SOLID`. While development is possible without adhering to them, following them leads to more efficient development and easier maintenance.

In fact, calling them "extremely" important is not an overstatement. Therefore, it is recommended to study, understand, and always consider these principles when creating and modifying new features. By the time you understand these principles, we will have become "software programmers" rather than just "coders - feature implementers."

SOLID is not meant literally as "solid" or "hard," but rather an acronym formed by the first letters of five important principles. The meaning of each letter is as follows. If you don't understand what this means just by looking at this part, that's normal.

- **S**: Single Responsibility Principle (단일 책임 원칙)
	- A class should have only one reason to change.
- **O**: Open-Closed Principle (개방-폐쇄 원칙)
	- Classes should be open for extension, but closed for modification.
- **L**: Liskov Substitution Principle (리스코프 치환 원칙)
	- Subtypes must be substitutable for their base types without altering the correctness of the program.
- **I**: Interface Segregation Principle (인터페이스 분리 원칙)
	- Interfaces should be segregated by role.
- **D**: Dependency Inversion Principle (의존성 역전 원칙)
	- The dependency between class implementations and their clients should be inverted by abstraction.
