Design patterns are important.
No matter how much you study, you sometimes end up violating patterns or mixing them incorrectly, creating inefficient code structures. That's why you have to keep reviewing and thinking about them.

Often, while developing, you may find yourself applying a design pattern without even realizing it.
However, the reason you need to study them is that a solution you create on your own is likely to have flaws or be inefficient. Design patterns are patterns that have been refined over a long period by countless developers, so they have the following advantages.
1. Just by knowing which pattern is used, you can get a good idea of the implementation structure. -> This facilitates communication among developers. Here, "developers" also includes "your past self."
2. They are efficient. Because they are structures that have been historically refined over a long time, not just thought up in a few hours, they have fewer problems.

Even experienced senior developers often misapply design patterns, even when they know them well. Therefore, not only juniors but also seniors must constantly study and strive to understand them. Furthermore, it's a good idea to research new patterns.

Before "studying" design patterns, you need to understand why these solutions, which anyone could implement, have been refined over a long period and established under the name of "patterns." The reason is that design patterns are not just simple methodologies.
To understand this, we first need to know the `SOLID` principles. Afterward, you will realize that all design patterns were ultimately created to implement various solutions **while adhering to the SOLID principles**, and you should develop the habit of always thinking about these principles. Even if a design pattern is applied, if the SOLID principles are not followed, it can be considered a misapplication of the pattern.

So, the next article will break down the `SOLID` principles one by one.
