Design patterns are important.
No matter how much you study, you sometimes end up violating patterns or mixing them incorrectly, creating inefficient code structures. That's why you have to keep reviewing and thinking about them.

As you build things, you often find yourself applying some design pattern without even realizing it.
However, the reason you need to study them is that something you create on your own is likely to have flaws or be inefficient. Design patterns have been refined by countless developers over a long period, so they offer the following advantages:
1. Just by knowing the pattern, you can get a good idea of the implementation's shape. -> This makes communication between developers easier. "Developers" here also includes "your past self."
2. They are efficient. Because they are structures that have been historically refined over a long time, not just something thought up in a few hours, they have fewer problems.

First, before "studying" design patterns, you need to understand why solutions that anyone could implement have been refined over a long period and established under the name of patterns. The reason is that design patterns are not just simple methodologies.
To understand this, we first need to know the `SOLID` principles. Afterward, you will realize that all design patterns were ultimately created to implement various solutions **while adhering to the SOLID principles**, and furthermore, you should make it a habit to always think about these principles.

So, the next article will break down the `SOLID` principles one by one.
