I am a Sebeolsik 390 user. As for why I use Sebeolsik, there isn't really a major reason. Thinking about it, the only reason I learned Dubeolsik was simply because it was the standard, and when I looked into why it became the standard, I didn't like what I found. However, Hangeul is inherently divided into initial, medial, and final consonants (chosung/jungsung/jongsung), and modern input systems can implement this perfectly. Sebeolsik is what I discovered while looking for an input method better suited to the structure of Hangeul. After the research of many people, it offers a typing method that is less fatiguing than Dubeolsik, and after a short effort, I was able to use Sebeolsik.

# Why Colemak?

The current standard for English keyboards is the `QWERTY` layout. Upon research, I found this to be a quite fatiguing input method and couldn't understand why it became the standard. I even read that it might have been standardized to intentionally slow down typing speeds on old mechanical typewriters, as fast typing would cause the machines to jam. Anyway, through the research of many people, much better keyboard layouts began to emerge, and `Colemak` is one of them.

There is one common characteristic among all non-standard keyboard layouts: **frequently used keys are placed in the center, assigned to the index and middle fingers**. This is likely intended to reduce hand fatigue by ergonomically analyzing hand structure. However, the biggest problem is that it is "non-standard." It takes time to learn, and even after getting used to it, it gets confused with the existing layout. The `Colemak` layout I chose had the great advantage of being the most similar to `QWERTY` among non-standard English layouts, resulting in a lower barrier to entry. By placing frequently used alphabets in less fatiguing positions while remaining similar, it was a great layout that could significantly improve typing speed and hand fatigue recovery once mastered. So, I started.

When I learned Sebeolsik, the first month was almost hell, but after a month, I got used to it, and by three months, it was more comfortable than Dubeolsik and I had fully adapted. I thought Colemak would be similar, but now, about a month into using it, I have given up on using Colemak.

# What Was the Problem?

As expected, I was getting used to `Colemak`. However, the biggest problem was that the **English QWERTY layout is adopted as the "default" in too many places, beyond just being a standard**. In the case of Hangeul, to be honest, it was very obvious when it would be used, and the places where it's used are very limited. Furthermore, the input method is very complex, so I relied on the PC's IME in almost every case. However, since the alphabet is not compositional, there were quite a few systems that implemented input directly without relying on an IME (especially on websites!). All these places are QWERTY-based, so as I started getting used to Colemak, I naturally typed in Colemak, made typos, and had to go through the process of deleting them. Also, there are cases where input is entered in QWERTY instead of Colemak when a "password field" is focused in the input method. For some unknown reason, when a `Hidden input` is focused, input is being entered as QWERTY by default. This is the same for Hangeul as well.
