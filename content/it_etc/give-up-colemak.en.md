I am a Sebeolsik 390 user. To be honest, there isn't a major reason why I use Sebeolsik. Looking back, the only reason I learned Dubeolsik was simply because it was the standard, and when I looked into why it became the standard, I didn't like what I found. However, Hangeul is inherently divided into initial, medial, and final consonants, and modern input systems can perfectly implement this. I looked into Sebeolsik because I wanted to use an input method that fits the structure of Hangeul better. After much research by many people, it features a typing method that allows for less fatigue than Dubeolsik, so I was able to start using Sebeolsik after a short period of effort.

# Why Colemak?

The current standard for English keyboard layouts is the `QWERTY` layout. Upon investigation, I found that this is also a quite fatiguing input method, and I couldn't understand why it became the standard. I even read an article suggesting it was standardized to intentionally make hands tired and slow down typing speed because old physical typewriters would break if typing was too fast. Anyway, after much research by many people, much better keyboard layouts began to emerge, and `Colemak` is one of them.

All non-standard keyboard layouts share one characteristic: they place **frequently used keys in the center, assigned to the index and middle fingers**. This seems to be intended to help reduce hand fatigue by analyzing the hand structure ergonomically. However, the biggest problem is that they are "non-standard." It takes time to learn, and even after getting used to it, it gets confused with the existing layout. The `Colemak` layout I chose had the great advantage of being the most similar to `QWERTY` among non-standard English layouts, resulting in a low barrier to entry. It was a good keyboard layout that placed frequently used letters in less fatiguing positions while remaining similar, significantly improving typing speed and hand fatigue recovery once you get used to it. That's why I started.

When I learned Sebeolsik, the first month was almost hell, but after a month, I got used to it, and by three months, it was more comfortable than Dubeolsik and I had fully adapted. I thought Colemak would be similar, but now, just over a month into using it, I have given up on using Colemak.

# What Was the Problem?

As expected, I was adapting to `Colemak`. However, the biggest problem was that the **English QWERTY keyboard layout is adopted as the "default" in too many places beyond just being a standard**. In the case of Hangeul, it was honestly very obvious when to use it, and its usage is very limited. Furthermore, the input method is very complex, so I relied on the PC's IME in almost every place. However, because the alphabet is not composed of combinations, there were quite a few systems that implemented input directly without relying on an IME (especially on websites!). Since all these places are based on QWERTY, as someone who started getting used to Colemak, I naturally typed in Colemak, made typos, and had to go through the process of deleting them.

Also, there are cases where input is entered in QWERTY instead of Colemak when the "password field" is focused in the input method. I'm not sure why, but when a `Hidden input` is focused, it takes input as QWERTY by default. This is the same for Hangeul... so why am I giving up? Come to think of it, if I just adapt a bit more, I think I could use it like Sebeolsik...?
